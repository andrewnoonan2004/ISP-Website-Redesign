2026–2027 MS curriculum · 30 credits

MS degree requirements and curriculum

The Master of Science in Intelligent Systems (MS) requires at least 30 graduate credits (10 three-credit courses), a minimum grade of B- or better in every required curriculum course, an overall 3.0 QPA, and completion of a faculty-advised MS Project (written report and oral defense before a 3-member faculty committee).

Theory courses 9 credits (3 courses) Choose at least 1 Statistics course (BIOST 2041, BIOST 2142 Applied Regression Analysis — replacing BIOST 2042 in 2026–2027 — BIOINF 2118, STAT 2131, or STAT 2132), at least 1 Computation & Algorithms course (CS 1511, CS 2110, CS 2150, or INFSCI 2591 / CS 2012), and 1 additional course from either Theory list.
Approved advanced courses 12 credits (4 courses) Four approved 2000-level advanced electives (including new 2026–2027 additions ISSP 2078 Deep Learning, ISSP 2125 Network Science, ISSP 2185 FATE in AI, ISSP 2419 Applied Legal Data Analytics & AI, and ISSP 2756 Principles of Data Mining). Note: ISSP 2000 (MS Research) does not replace required advanced electives.
Academic standing & MS Project 30 credits total Earn B- or better in every required curriculum course, maintain at least a 3.0 QPA overall, and defend a written MS Project report before a 3-member faculty committee (TODO: confirm whether 2 or all 3 committee members must be ISP faculty).

General Intelligent Systems Track — 2026–2027 MS courses (30 credits)

Complete 3 Core courses (9 credits) — required INFSCI 2440 / ISSP 2440 (Artificial Intelligence) plus two additional core courses from ISSP 2071 (Human Language Technologies), INFSCI 2595 (Machine Learning), ISSP 2180 / CS 2770 (Computer Vision), ISSP 2130 / INFSCI 2140 (Information Storage and Retrieval), or INFSCI 2160 (Data Mining) — along with 3 Theory courses (9 credits) (at least 1 Statistics, at least 1 Computation & Algorithms, and 1 additional course from either Theory list) and 4 Approved Advanced Courses (12 credits).

Curriculum tracks and academic standing

The 2026–2027 MS program outlines two curriculum tracks: the General Intelligent Systems Track and the Biomedical Informatics Track. Per the program guide and 2026–2027 Pitt Graduate Catalog (catoid=242), students complete at least 30 credits, earn a grade of B- or better in each required curriculum course, maintain an overall QPA of at least 3.0, and complete an MS project.

Advising and course planning

New MS students are generally assigned to the ISP Director (Peter Brusilovsky) as their initial advisor for the first year and may switch to any ISP faculty advisor by obtaining their approval and submitting the Graduate Change of Advisor form (PDF) ↗ (copying the ISP administrator). Meet with your advisor each term before registration so the ISP administrator can lift your advising hold.

Your MS project and ISSP 2000

The MS project is a faculty-advised research or practical AI project—not a formal MS thesis (publishing a paper is encouraged but not required). It comprises:

  1. Significant research, design, or development work demonstrating your skills with modern AI technologies.
  2. A written project report.
  3. An oral defense/presentation before your three-member faculty committee, chaired by your ISP faculty advisor.

Start early: most students finish the project in their final semester, but waiting until the final semester to begin often delays graduation. You may optionally register for ISSP 2000 (MS Research) with your advisor to allocate credit hours toward project work (the program guide recommends taking no more than two regular lecture courses alongside 3 credits of ISSP 2000 during an intensive project semester; ISSP 2000 does not count as an elective).

Before inviting committee members: the 2026 MS guide contains two different statements on whether two or all three committee members must be ISP faculty (TODO: confirm). Ask your advisor and the ISP administrator to confirm the committee composition that applies to your project before inviting members.

MS scholarships & conference travel

Pitt2Pitt Scholarship, SCI awards & travel grants

The MS in Intelligent Systems (MSISP) is a verified participating program in the Pitt2Pitt Scholarship for Pitt undergraduates and alumni (up to $7,500/year full-time or $3,750/year part-time). MS students can also apply for SCI Graduate Scholarships (King, Howard, and Smith awards), match with awards in PittFund$Me, and apply for $750–$1,000/year in ISP Student Travel Grants when presenting an accepted paper or poster at an AI conference.
