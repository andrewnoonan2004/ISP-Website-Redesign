PhD degree requirements and curriculum

The Doctor of Philosophy in Intelligent Systems (PhD) requires at least 72 graduate credits across core and advanced coursework and mentored research, a minimum grade of B- or better in every required curriculum course, an overall 3.0 QPA in full (non-provisional) standing, and completion of the Preliminary Examination Project, Comprehensive Examination, and Doctoral Dissertation.

Requirement category Credits & milestone 2026–2027 requirement details
First-year research sequence 7–9 credits (Year 1) Complete ISSP 2020 (First Year Mentored Research), INFSCI 3005 / CS 2001 (Introduction to Research in Intelligent Systems), and ISSP 2030 (Archival Primary Research) culminating in a written research paper and 30-minute Spring ISP Forum presentation for the Preliminary Examination.
Core courses 9+ credits General Track: Required ISSP 2160 / CS 2710 (Foundations of Artificial Intelligence) + choose two core courses from ISSP 2170 / CS 2750 (Machine Learning), ISSP 2071 (Human Language Technologies), ISSP 2230 / CS 2731 (Introductory NLP), or ISSP 2180 / CS 2770 (Computer Vision) (TODO: confirm exact 2026–2027 PhD General Core “choose two” list against catoid=242).
Biomedical Informatics Track: ISSP 2083 / BIOINF 2032, ISSP 2016 / BIOINF 2070, ISSP 2160 / CS 2710, two additional BMI core selections, and the 3-credit teaching practicum BIOINF 3998.
Theory courses 9 credits (3 courses) Choose at least 1 Statistics course (BIOST 2041, BIOST 2142 Applied Regression Analysis — replacing BIOST 2042 in 2026–2027 — BIOINF 2118, STAT 2131, or STAT 2132), at least 1 Computation & Algorithms course (CS 2110 or CS 2150), and 1 additional course from either Theory list.
Advanced electives & doctoral research Remaining credits to 72 total Approved 2000- and 3000-level advanced courses (including ISSP 2165, ISSP 3120, ISSP 3535, ISSP 3565, ISSP 2078, ISSP 2125, ISSP 2185, ISSP 2419, ISSP 2756) and doctoral dissertation credits (ISSP 3000, FTDJ 0000, SMSJ 0000).
Milestones & standing 72 credits total Maintain B- or better in required curriculum courses and a 3.0 QPA; complete the Preliminary Exam, Comprehensive Exam (depth in 3 AI subareas), Dissertation Proposal & Defense, annual progress review, and ISP Forum attendance (all but 2 talks/term).

General Intelligent Systems Track & first-year sequence — 2026–2027 PhD courses (72 credits)

Complete the first-year preliminary research sequence (ISSP 2020, INFSCI 3005 / CS 2001, and ISSP 2030), ISSP 2160 / CS 2710 (Foundations of Artificial Intelligence) plus two additional core courses (TODO: confirm exact 2026–2027 PhD General Track Core “choose two” list against 2026–2027 PhD catalog catoid=242 ↗), 3 Theory courses (9 credits) (at least 1 Statistics, at least 1 Computation & Algorithms, and 1 additional Theory course), approved 2000- and 3000-level advanced courses, and doctoral dissertation research (ISSP 3000, FTDJ 0000, SMSJ 0000).

Curriculum tracks and academic standing

The 2026–2027 PhD program outlines two tracks: the General Intelligent Systems Track and the Biomedical Informatics Track. Per the program guide and 2026–2027 Pitt Graduate Catalog (catoid=242), doctoral students complete at least 72 credits (coursework and research), earn a grade of B- or better in each required curriculum course, and maintain a QPA of at least 3.0 in full (non-provisional) standing.

Research and advising

New students are generally assigned to the ISP Director (Peter Brusilovsky) for their first year. Your ongoing advisor must be a member of the ISP faculty. To change advisors, obtain approval from your prospective advisor and submit the Graduate Change of Advisor form (PDF) ↗, copying the ISP administrator (hld46@pitt.edu).

Milestones and annual review

Work with your advisor to plan your preliminary examination project (developed across ISSP 2020 and ISSP 2030 with a 30-minute Spring ISP Forum presentation), comprehensive examination, dissertation proposal, and defense. Each year, students submit a self-evaluation and review their Academic Advising Report (AAR) alongside their advisor’s evaluation; the ISP faculty and Director review progress and send an annual summary letter. Under University statute of limitations rules, PhD requirements must be completed within 10 years of initial registration (or 8 years if awarded credit for an appropriate Master’s degree).

Doctoral fellowships & assistantships

SCI & Provost fellowships, GSR appointments & travel grants

Incoming PhD applicants are automatically considered for SCI Predoctoral Fellowships. Continuing and incoming doctoral students are supported through Graduate Research Assistantships (GSR) with ISP faculty PIs, Provost Predoctoral Fellowships, Summer GSA Fellowships, the Elizabeth U. Baranger Predoctoral Fellowship, the Provost’s Dissertation Completion Fellowship, the DBMI Training Grant, and $750–$1,000/year ISP Student Travel Grants.

Explore PhD fellowships and funding →

ISP forums

ISP Forums take place on alternating Fridays from 12:30–1:30 p.m. in 5317 Sennott Square. To remain in good standing and maintain eligibility for Provost Fellowships, SCI funding, summer GSA funding, and ISP Travel Grants, PhD students are expected to attend and sign in at all but two (2) scheduled Forum talks per term (and present at least once a year, or after receiving an ISP Travel Grant). Notify the ISP director and administrator in advance if extenuating circumstances prevent attendance.

Dissertation and summer milestone study (FTDJ & SMSJ)

  • Full-Time Dissertation Study (FTDJ): Provides full-time status at reduced fee levels once you have completed 72 degree credits, finished all required courses, and are working toward your Comprehensive Examination.
  • Summer Milestone Study (SMSJ): Supports PhD students working on milestones over the summer who are not yet eligible for FTDJ.

Confirm eligibility with your advisor and the ISP administrator before requesting a permission number ↗.
