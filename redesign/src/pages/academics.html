2026–2027 curriculum overview

Degree requirements at a glance

The Intelligent Systems Program offers two multidisciplinary graduate degrees in artificial intelligence and biomedical informatics: the 30-credit Master of Science (MS) and the 72-credit Doctor of Philosophy (PhD). Both degrees offer a General Intelligent Systems Track and a Biomedical Informatics Track, and require a minimum grade of B- or better in every required curriculum course and an overall 3.0 QPA.

Scholarships, fellowships & assistantships

Funding your graduate study in Intelligent Systems

PhD funding: Incoming doctoral applicants are automatically considered for SCI Predoctoral Fellowships, and PhD students are supported through Graduate Research Assistantships (GSR) with ISP faculty PIs, Provost Predoctoral Fellowships, Summer GSA Fellowships, the Elizabeth U. Baranger Predoctoral Fellowship, the Provost’s Dissertation Completion Fellowship, and the DBMI Training Grant.

MS scholarships & conference travel: The MS in Intelligent Systems (MSISP) participates in the Pitt2Pitt Scholarship (up to $7,500/year full-time or $3,750/year part-time for Pitt undergraduates and alumni), alongside SCI Graduate Scholarships (King, Howard, and Smith awards) and PittFund$Me. All active MS and PhD students can apply for $750–$1,000/year in ISP Student Travel Grants to present accepted papers or posters at AI conferences.
