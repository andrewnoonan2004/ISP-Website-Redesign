PhD fellowships, research assistantships, and training grants

Doctoral students in the Intelligent Systems Program are supported through a combination of school and university fellowships, faculty-sponsored research assistantships, and federal training grants:

  • School of Computing and Information (SCI) Predoctoral Fellowships: A limited number of SCI Predoctoral Fellowships are offered by ISP for the fall and spring terms. Incoming PhD applicants are automatically considered by the admissions committee—you do not need to submit a separate fellowship application. Award decisions for fall admission are typically made by a committee of ISP faculty members in February or early March and communicated in your admission letter (legacy priority financial aid application deadline: January 15; TODO: confirm current entry-term priority deadline and slot count with sciadmit@pitt.edu).
  • Provost Predoctoral Fellowships: Offered competitively by the University of Pittsburgh across graduate programs. Typically, ISP doctoral students are nominated to apply for the Provost Predoctoral Fellowship after their second year of graduate study, with award decisions announced in early March.
  • Graduate Research Assistantships (GSR / Graduate Research Awards): Funded through sponsored research projects administered by individual ISP faculty members. Award decisions are made by the principal investigator (PI) in consultation with the program. Prospective and current PhD students are encouraged to explore faculty research in the Faculty Explorer and review ISP research groups to connect with PIs whose projects align with their interests.
  • Summer GSA Fellowships: Two Graduate Student Assistantship (GSA) fellowships are available competitively during the summer term. Decisions are made by a committee of program faculty members (typically in February or early March), and no special application is required (TODO: confirm current summer fellowship availability).
  • Elizabeth U. Baranger Predoctoral Fellowship: An SCI fellowship for incoming STEM PhD students supporting women in STEM, providing a 12-month stipend, full tuition scholarship, and individual health insurance (review SCI scholarship details ↗).
  • Provost’s Dissertation Completion Fellowship: Supports advanced University of Pittsburgh doctoral students in their final year of dissertation writing (review Provost’s Dissertation Year Fellowship ↗).
  • DBMI Training Grant (Biomedical Informatics): ISP applicants who have earned an MD and are U.S. citizens or permanent residents are eligible for support through a training grant to the Center / Department of Biomedical Informatics (explore DBMI Training Program funding ↗; TODO: confirm current DBMI training program URL and eligibility).

Maintaining PhD funding eligibility & reduced milestone tuition

To remain in good standing and maintain eligibility for Provost Fellowships, SCI funding, summer GSA fellowships, and ISP Travel Grants, PhD students must maintain a QPA of at least 3.0 (with B- or better in required curriculum courses) and attend and sign in at all but two (2) scheduled ISP Forum talks per term. Advanced doctoral students who have completed 72 credits and all required coursework may also qualify for reduced fee levels through Full-Time Dissertation Study (FTDJ) or Summer Milestone Study (SMSJ).

MS scholarships, Pitt2Pitt, and financial aid

Setting clear expectations for Master’s funding: Per School of Computing and Information graduate admissions guidance, doctoral students are commonly funded through fellowships and GSR/TA/GSA appointments, whereas program-funded assistantships and full fellowships for Master of Science (MS) students are rare. MS students primarily finance their degree through tuition scholarships, endowed school awards, hourly campus employment, or external sponsorship:

  • Pitt2Pitt Scholarship (Verified MSISP Participating Program): The Master of Science in Intelligent Systems (MSISP) is an official participating program in the University of Pittsburgh’s Pitt2Pitt Scholarship ↗ for graduating Pitt undergraduates and Pitt alumni. Eligible students can receive up to $7,500 per academic year for full-time enrollment ($2,500 per term for 9 or more credits) or $3,750 per academic year for part-time enrollment ($1,250 per term for at least 4.5 credits). Confirm current term limits, GPA tiers, and eligible entry terms (TODO: confirm) with SCI admissions at sciadmit@pitt.edu.
  • SCI Graduate Scholarships: The School of Computing and Information administers several endowed scholarships open to graduate students (view SCI scholarships ↗):
    • Barbara E. King Scholarship Award: Supports undergraduate and graduate students in SCI.
    • Russell “Rusty” A. Howard II Memorial Scholarship: Supports graduate students enrolled in the School of Computing and Information.
    • Jerry and Cathy Smith Endowed Scholarship Fund: Supports enrolled SCI graduate students.
  • PittFund$Me Scholarship Database: Once admitted to the University of Pittsburgh, students gain access to PittFund$Me (via My Pitt ↗), a searchable scholarship matching portal for University-wide and external awards.
  • Conference Travel Support for MS Researchers: MS students who have an accepted paper or poster at an AI-related conference are fully eligible to apply for the $750–$1,000 ISP Student Travel Grant described below.

ISP student conference travel grants ($750–$1,000)

Once per academic year, ISP graduate students (MS and PhD) are eligible to apply for the ISP Student Travel Grant. The grant provides between $750 and $1,000 in travel support, based on need, for AI-related conferences where the student has had a paper or poster accepted (TODO: confirm current annual travel grant budget availability with the ISP administrator before booking travel).

How to apply (at least 1 month before the conference)

Email the following three items to the ISP Director (Peter Brusilovsky) and ISP Administrator (Heidi Davis, hld46@pitt.edu) at least one month prior to the conference:

  1. Conference details: Name, location, and dates of the AI-related conference.
  2. Acceptance confirmation: A copy of the official acceptance letter or email from the conference for your paper or poster.
  3. Advisor approval: A recommendation letter or email from your ISP faculty advisor confirming approval of your participation.

Approval, reimbursement via Bobbie Coyne, and ISP Forum presentation

  • Award letter: The ISP Director reviews applications and issues an official award notification letter stating the approved funding amount.
  • Post-trip reimbursement: Reimbursements are processed after you return from the conference. Submit your award letter and travel expense documentation to Bobbie Coyne (bjc134@pitt.edu) so the travel and business expense report can be routed to ISP for the account number and Director’s signature.
  • ISP Forum talk: Travel grant recipients are expected to present their conference paper or poster at an upcoming ISP Forum (alternating Fridays, 12:30–1:30 p.m., 5317 Sennott Square).

Supplemental travel grant petitions

If ISP has sufficient funding beyond its guaranteed annual travel budget (TODO: confirm current-year supplemental budget), the program will consider student petitions for additional travel support covering:

  1. Participation in mentoring events such as conference doctoral consortiums or CRA grad cohorts.
  2. Serving as first author on multiple accepted conference papers in a single academic year.

To petition for a supplemental travel grant, follow the same one-month advance application steps above.

External fellowships and official Pitt funding portals

Many forms of external support are available to graduate students in artificial intelligence and biomedical informatics, including the National Science Foundation (NSF) Graduate Research Fellowship Program (GRFP), the Fulbright Awards Program, and industry research fellowships. ISP strongly encourages eligible prospective and current students to apply for external fellowships—both to expand funding flexibility and to earn national recognition on your academic record.
